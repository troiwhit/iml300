Project 01 · Interactive Audio + Visual

What Are You Listening To?

An interactive audio-visual fashion collage inspired by the TikTok trend of imagining what someone listens to based on their outfit. I took some screenshots of some different outfits/aesthitics and grouped each one into groups: "bohemian," "goth," and "simple/clean-girl." I input three different songs that I thought reflected each aesthetic. Each song hovers over its corresponding aesthetic in the form of an audio wave buttion that you can click.

Project 02 · Hypernarrative

The Sunken Place

Originally inspired by the Sunken Place sequence in Jordan Peele's "Get Out" specifically regarding the main character, Chris, and his fractured consiousness. However, for this project I made it more personal and invited users inside my own fractured consciousness while also posiitoning them as an observer. The user enocunters my memories and images from my life from a distance, creating that feeling of being separated from your own body and unable to intervene with what happens to it. There is no story to solve, but each choice reveals separate pieces of the same psychological experience -- being conscious, watching, remembering, but still not able to act.

SLATE Movement Casting

I used Claude.ai to create a website tool for dance/talent agencies to connect movement artists with directors and choreographers for the projects they need dancers for. The website is called SLATE Movement Casting. It would almost act as the talent agent with programmed scouting, matching, and assessing dancers' movement styles to match with a creative project's vision.

The Unproductive Desktop

I read American Artist's "Black Gooey Universe" and prompted Claude.ai to make an interface where attempts at efficiency create opacity, friction, and refusal rather than successful completion. It's called "The Unproductive Desktop" and it begins as a clean productivity dashboard, but each click releases black residue that covers buttons, reverses progress, and replaces tasks with questions. What happens when a productivity system doesn't treat speed, control, cleanliness, and completion as neutral? Refusing productivity is also a way to use a computer.

Soft Index

This website is meant to articulate Laurel Schwulst's idea that a website can be an evolving, personal, self-authored space that's shaped by curiosity, exploration, and change. "Soft Index" is a website where users can always reorganize the page, becoming part of the architecture of the website themselves.

We Contain Multitudes

I was inspired by Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie's TED Talk "The Danger of a Single Story." In my previous work, I've actually dealt with this topic, specifically regarding the consequent dangers darker-skinned Black women endure at the hands of media's very monolithic representation of them. In film and television, darker-skinned Black women are cast as the side character, subservient, angry, sad, perpetually hard-working, gripped with trauma, etc. And although, these can be our realities, we are more than just that. We are also lively, vulnerable, soft, silly, capable of love, affectionate, warm, and could very much be the center of a story. This experiment is called "We Contain Multitudes" and would start off with a collage of images of darker-skinned Black women in the roles they have been regulated to. When you press, "Look Beyond the Frame," the initial collage is disrupted with other images of darker-skinned women that combat the monolithic ones from before.

Year Zero

For this experiment, I wanted to explore how a digital creature can transform while still keeping traces of its past. I was inspired by Auriea Harvey's "Year Zero" that explores transformation, memory, mythology, and the distinction between physical and digital worlds. I'm interested in blending organic and sculptural forms so users can shape an evolving artwork through interaction rather than following a fixed narrative.

Blackness for Sale

Inspired by Mendi + Keith Obadike's "Blackness for Sale. Similar to how they created a listing on ebay for their "Blackness heirloom," I prompted Loveable.ai to build an advertisement for a purchasable "Blackness," presenting its benefits through bright colors and exciting promotional language. The warnings, however, appear in small red lettering like how some ads minimize a product's drawbacks. I wanted to explore the contradiction between celebrating Black identity and Black contributions to mainstream culture as desirable commodities while treating the actual Black body and community unfairly. The website asks viewers to confront what consumer culture highlights, appropriates, and deliberately pushes out of sight.
